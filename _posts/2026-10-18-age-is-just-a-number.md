It was around 8 in the morning. I was looking at the church through my window while sipping my coffee. It was a bright, sunny day, but I had a long to-do list ahead of me. I was working towards a fast-approaching deadline, and there was so much to get done.

Then I decided to check my WhatsApp messages and reply to the ones I had been putting off. One particular message caught my attention. It was about a trip for expats. Out of curiosity, I clicked on it.

It was a trip to the historical Veveří Castle, including a boat ride and some hiking. What really caught my attention was the **boat ride**. The castle, built in the mid-13th century, is a historical monument, and I had always wanted to visit it by ferry through the Brno Reservoir.

Without thinking twice, I signed up! I had no idea who my companions would be.

As I rushed towards the dock from where the boat was scheduled to leave, I barely had time to find out anything about the organisers or the participants.

When I finally arrived, I found around 15–20 people from Brno and the surrounding areas who had joined the trip.

Guess what the average age of the group was?

**60 years…** (I might have reduced it a little! You know, average…)

Suddenly, I felt like a kid again.

Yes, they were all senior citizens, and many of them were great-grandparents. But here comes the most surprising part: this trip was part of their English-learning curriculum!

Yes, they were learning English — out of their own interest, with no real need for it in their daily lives. They were simply doing it to try something new, keep themselves engaged, and continue learning.

How many people do we know who make an effort to learn something new at this stage of life? Hardly any!

I was amazed, not only by the beautiful scenery during the ferry ride from the Brno Reservoir to the castle, but also by the enthusiasm of these people. They were so open-minded and full of life. They could climb stairs, hike for more than 5 kilometres, and practise a foreign language with their friends.

They were also genuinely interested in talking to me. They were curious about my culture, my country, and my hobbies.

As they say, *"What is more important — the journey or the destination?"* Perhaps the answer is **the company**.

And it was absolutely true that day. The people around me were so energising that I didn't even realise how quickly the time had passed.

But one person, in particular, touched my heart.

## A Stranger Who Reminded Me of My Grandfather

Among the group was an elderly man, around 86 years old. He was a great-grandfather and a widower.

He told me about his childhood, when he used to run 6 kilometres to the dam and take a dip in the Brno Reservoir every day. He was a little upset that he could no longer run there, although he still managed to swim in the reservoir sometimes.

As we slowly started bonding, something dawned on me.

I had lost my grandfather when he was around 70. If he were still alive, he would have been around this gentleman's age.

That realisation made me feel unexpectedly grateful — grateful to God and to this stranger who barely knew me, yet somehow made me feel as if I were with my grandfather again.

Before I go any further, let me tell you a little about my grandfather.

## My Grandfather, My Childhood, My Memories

My grandfather was a quiet and kind person. He loved gardening and had a garden full of fruits and vegetables. He was an English teacher at a nearby government school.

During my summer holidays, I would stay at his old, enormous villa with a big courtyard. He used to teach me English there.

He also practised yoga and read the *Bhagavad Gita*. At the end of his yoga session, he would lie down for *Shavasana*, which was supposed to be five minutes of relaxation. But somehow, those five minutes would turn into a deep sleep lasting an hour!

He always pampered me. And he was never shy about admitting it, whether to me or to anyone else. That was one of the things that made him so special to me.

I remember one Diwali when I made a rangoli in the courtyard. A rangoli is a colourful design made using coloured powders.

My grandfather was so proud and happy that he refused to let anyone clean it for ten whole days! Eventually, it became a complete mess, covered with dirt and scattered by the wind.

But he didn't care.

Whenever anyone visited our house, he would proudly say, *"See how beautiful it is! My granddaughter made it!"*

I never told him how happy that made me feel.

Sometimes, adults say or do things without realising how deeply they can influence a child's life. Even today, that memory brings a smile to my face.

As I grew older, I became busy with my studies, and my visits to his home became less frequent.

Then, one fine day, we learned that he had pancreatic cancer.

Everyone was devastated.

He was someone who hardly ate anything other than lauki (bottle gourd), which I hated then and still don't particularly like now, spinach, and fruits. Junk food was practically a crime in his eyes!

I am not discouraging anyone from eating healthy food. But life is unfair sometimes, and we have to learn to live with that reality.

A few months later, he passed away. Thankfully, he did not suffer too much.

But even today, I regret not seeing him one last time. I wasn't brave enough to see someone I loved so dearly in his final moments. I wasn't mature enough to understand what death really meant.

It was the first time I had lost someone so close to me.

There is something else I regret, too: **I never took him to the sea.**

He would often say, *"I have never seen the sea or the ocean. I want to go to the sea at least once."*

After his death, I visited the sea many times. And every single time, I wished he were there with me.

Perhaps he is there somewhere, watching me and feeling proud!

## Back to the Present

Now, coming back to that Sunday trip.

I spent most of my time, knowingly or unknowingly, with this grandfatherly gentleman.

He was visiting the castle after a very long time. What struck me was that, despite his age, his curiosity about the world was still alive.

As we grow older, many of us seem to lose our curiosity. We begin to take things for granted. We stop asking questions and exploring the world around us.

But not him.

He was curious about the castle's history and read almost every information board explaining the significance of the place. He would then share what he had learned with me.

He had been a civil engineer, and at one point, he showed me a hospital care unit that he had helped build around 30 years ago.

He was so proud of it.

And why shouldn't he be?

Imagine contributing to something that continues to serve people decades later. That must be a wonderful feeling.

We eventually had lunch and took a bus back towards Brno.

His stop came before mine, and he had to leave in a hurry. I couldn't even say a proper goodbye to him.

Maybe that's for the best.

Because perhaps, just perhaps, I will meet him again…

## What We Leave Behind

That Sunday began like any other day. I had a long to-do list, a looming deadline, and every intention of spending the day being productive.

But a spontaneous decision took me on a journey I hadn't planned for — one that reminded me of my grandfather, my childhood, and the little things in life that we often fail to appreciate.

I went looking for a boat ride to a castle. I returned with something much more precious: a memory, a connection, and a gentle reminder that curiosity has no age limit.

We often measure our days by how much we accomplish. We worry about deadlines, plans, and all the things we haven't finished yet.

But sometimes, the most memorable moments are the ones that were never on our to-do lists.

And sometimes, a complete stranger can make us feel closer to someone we have lost.

As the saying goes:

*"People may not remember who you are or what you did, but they will remember how you made them feel."*

That Sunday, an elderly stranger made me feel something I hadn't felt in a long time.

For a little while, I felt I had my grandfather back.

**Thank you for your attention!**
